## Teks klip baru
- **p2_0** (A, 159 kar.) Part One ended in the north, with Vitebsk gone and the Fourth Army falling back to the Berezina. This is the southern jaw: Bobruisk, and the German Ninth Army.
- **p2_1** (A, 68 kar.) Stalin, according to the usual account, wanted a single main attack.
- **p2_2** (A, 176 kar.) It had not started well. On the twenty-third, a first assault near Rogachev was thrown back with heavy losses, and Rokossovsky ordered the artillery preparation to be repeated.
- **p2_3** (A, 214 kar.) In the north, the Third Army, under Alexander Gorbatov, pushes towards the Berezina. On the twenty-sixth, the Forty-Eighth Army takes Zhlobin, and the Ninth Tank Corps is sent through the gap, into the German rear.
- **p2_4** (A, 444 kar.) The Sixty-Fifth Army is led by Pavel Batov, a general known for crossing rivers. Rokossovsky is counting on him to get through the marsh, on log roads and marsh shoes. On his flank, the Twenty-Eighth Army guards the advance of Pliyev's group: the First Mechanised Corps and the Fourth Guards Cavalry Corps. Two historians called Pliyev a great practitioner of cavalry operations in adverse terrain. In nineteen forty-four, he gets plenty of it.
- **p2_6** (A, 200 kar.) Inside the pocket are five divisions of the Thirty-Fifth Corps, parts of the Forty-First Panzer Corps, and the 20th Panzer Division. Soviet bombers strike the Ninth Army's communications headquarters.
- **p2_7** (A, 92 kar.) The breakout hits the line of the Soviet 356th Rifle Division, part of the Sixty-Fifth Army.
- **p2_8** (A, 198 kar.) One account puts the Ninth Army's casualties at nearly eighty thousand, sixty-five thousand of them prisoners. Russian archives record seven thousand and sixty-one Soviet soldiers killed or missing.
- **p2_9** (A, 125 kar.) In the north, a corps is gone. In the south, an army. Between them, the German Fourth Army is still moving west. Next: Minsk.
- **p2_B1** (B, 194 kar.) The Ninth Army knows this war. In nineteen forty-three, it was the northern spearhead at Kursk. By nineteen forty-four it is exhausted, defending Bobruisk with a few badly needed reinforcements.
- **p2_B2** (B, 363 kar.) Bobruisk has seen this once before, in reverse. German troops captured it on the twenty-eighth of June, nineteen forty-one. In its old citadel they built Dulag 131, one of the largest camps for Soviet prisoners of war. An estimated thirty to forty thousand Red Army soldiers died there. Some twenty thousand of the town's Jews were shot and buried in mass graves.
- **p2_B3** (B, 180 kar.) The city the Red Army enters in nineteen forty-four is in ruins. Its population has fallen from eighty-four thousand in nineteen thirty-nine to twenty-eight thousand after the war.
- **p2_B4** (B, 154 kar.) About forty per cent of the Ninth Army was destroyed that summer. It will be rebuilt, with units brought from Italy, and will defend Warsaw by the autumn.
